# Revisiting Mynt: the cardboard appraisal

*A follow-up to my Mynt/GCash valuation.*

![Valuation ranges from the Mynt prospectus against the PHP 6.60 offer price. Two DCFs and a book-value identity cluster near PHP 2.00; the peer-multiple route is the only bar that reaches the PHP 10.00 ceiling set in July 2026, since superseded. The cerise marker is the 6.60 offer price of 2 October 2026, sitting above most of the model range. Source: author's analysis of the Mynt prospectus and offering range.](assets/mynt-cardboard/football_field.png)

> **Disclosure:** I use AI as a writing partner to refine my prose and structure, but the ideas, analysis, and first drafts originate entirely with me.

Everyone knows a piece of cardboard printed with a picture of a Pokémon or an NBA star can sell for five figures. We do not call those buyers delusional. A rare card is scarce, and the story attached to it is real money to whoever owns it. The appraiser pricing the cardboard isn’t wrong. He is just answering a different question than the buyer.

My July model said Mynt was worth about two pesos a share. I stood behind that number through September, when the company itself kept saying not to expect ten and the offered range slid down through seven. On October 2 BlackRock and Fidelity anchored at 6.60 with demand reportedly several times the offering (!). The floor of my appraisal never moved. The market simply chose to pay above it, however constrained by a force called “reality”.

Today it listed. Mynt rang the opening bell at the PSE, with PBBM leading the ceremony, trading under the ticker GCASH. It is the largest IPO in Philippine history: about P60.9 billion raised, roughly US$967 million, the largest Southeast Asian listing of 2026, with market cap near P441.5 billion at debut.

The trading itself was a whimper wearing a tuxedo. Shares opened at 6.64 against the 6.60 offer, touched 6.76, averaged 6.65, and closed dead flat at 6.60. Some 650 million shares changed hands for about P4.33 billion. Heavy volume, no direction. Reuters put it mildly: “edges higher after record IPO.”

That flat close is the whole argument in a single candle. My model and the exchanges were never disagreeing about numbers. I asked what the thing is worth; they asked what someone will pay. That second question has an intangible element I never modeled: 70% of the offering went to institutions chasing something scarce carrying a story, a profitable consumer franchise in a country of 120 million where half the adults use the app monthly. Scarcity plus story is a distribution problem, and my model has no row for either.

Notice what day one did NOT do. It didn’t pop, which would have said the story trade was still running. It didn’t sink, which would have said the anchors had already paid for their exit. Flat at the offer on huge volume is the sound of a stock waiting for its next fact. Lockups freeze much of the float; when they expire, whoever the clearing price assumed would hold stops being them.

So here is the forecast: within six months, by April 2027, the stock trades closer to my model’s band than to 6.60, and I write a third post. Or it doesn’t, and the institutions were right about something I was blind to. Day one decides neither way. Fine. The interesting signal was never anybody’s valuation. It was a room full of people with real information deciding the cardboard price was not the interesting number, and getting what they wanted. The market agreed at 6.60, then went home.
